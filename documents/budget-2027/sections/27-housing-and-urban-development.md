# 27. Housing and Urban Development

<!-- p. 57 -->

A secure house and a healthy urban environment are the most fundamental basis for the progress of a nation and its human resources. Our expectation is to uplift the living standards of the people by planning economic, social and physical development in an integrated manner, while protecting the natural and built environment.

• It is expected to construct 70,000 houses under the "A Place of One's Own – A Beautiful Life" programme to resolve the housing problem of low-income

<!-- p. 58 -->

earners. Under this programme, which has already commenced, it is expected to construct 10,000 new houses in 2027. The housing assistance provided per house under this programme will be increased from Rs. 1 million to Rs. 1.5 million. It is proposed to increase the provision currently allocated for this purpose to Rs. 15,000 million.

• 13,000 families displaced due to the armed conflict that prevailed in the North and East have still not been resettled. It is the target of our Government to complete the resettlement of these families within 2 years. Rs. 6,000 million has been allocated for this purpose, and it is proposed to allocate a further Rs. 4,000 million.

• The programme to construct 10,000 houses, launched with the cooperation of the Government of India to resolve the housing problem of the plantation community, will be further accelerated. • It is proposed to allocate provisions of Rs. 1,000 million for providing houses to those in areas such as Abaranchimulla, Serakkuliya, Mangalapura, Kandasamy Nagar and Nithya Nagar in Vavuniya, Pediyafarm, Polonnaruwa, Mullaitivu, Tissamaharama, Divulapitiya, Dimbulagala and Hennanigala- South who were affected by the Ditwah disaster but did not become entitled to a house due to the existing criteria.

• The owners of a large number of housing projects, which were commenced in the years 2015 - 19 on small financial assistance of Rs. 20,000 and Rs. 40,000 and were abandoned midway, have been put to great hardship. It is proposed to allocate Rs. 5,000 million for financial assistance of up to Rs. 500,000 to eligible beneficiaries to complete the remaining construction work of half-completed houses.

• It is expected to allocate Rs. 90,000 million through the Supplementary Estimate for the construction of houses under the Ditwah Housing Project. • A requirement of 50,000 houses has been identified for urban low-income earners, of which construction of more than 16,000 houses has already been fully completed and construction of a further 5,000 houses has been partially completed. The partially completed housing projects at Torrington, Colombage Mawatha, Kolonnawa, Peliyagoda, Moratuwa, Maharagama and Dematagoda will be completed in 2027. It is also proposed to commence the construction of

<!-- p. 59 -->

new housing complexes in the Meethotamulla and Wilgoda, Kurunegala areas. Rs. 26,820 million has been allocated for all these housing projects. • The National Housing Development Authority has planned to implement 2 new middle-income housing projects in the Maligawatta and Yakkala areas in 2027 on a pre-sale basis.

• Initiatives have already commenced to transform ten towns, Jaffna, Matale, Hatton, Batticaloa, Chilaw, Thambuttegama, Vavuniya, Eheliyagoda, Badulla, and Matara, into well-organized and systematic urban centers, as part of a city branding and tourism-focused urban development program. A sum of Rs.

2,700 million has been allocated for this purpose. • In addition, it is proposed to allocate Rs. 5,000 million for the year 2027 for 19 towns, namely Trincomalee, Kantale, Kalmunai, Dehiwala, Kalutara, Panadura, Matugama, Maharagama, Ratnapura, Kegalle, Bandarawela, Ella, Galle, Akuressa, Kurunegala, Puttalam, Nattandiya, Kiribathgoda, and Nuwara Eliya over and above the provisions already made for development activities through project estimates totaling Rs. 65,000 million.

• The need to efficiently maintain drainage systems, carry out emergency operations during floods, and systematically maintain small canals and drainage systems in order to minimize flood risk in urban low-lying areas is increasing. For this, steps will be taken to obtain multipurpose machinery worth Rs. 1,250 million for the Sri Lanka Land Development Corporation, and it is proposed to provide Rs. 625 million of this as Government equity capital.

• It is expected to encourage the Government, the private sector, banks and finance companies to jointly provide housing to resolve the housing problem of families working in the public, semi-government and private sectors and those engaged in their own businesses. Under this programme, we expect to provide the facilities required to construct 10,000 apartment units in major cities over the next 5 years.

Urban Flood Control • Urban flooding is caused by the failure to properly maintain the main canals in the city of Colombo and its suburbs in a timely manner, as well as the insufficient capacity of the canals that carry rainwater. In addition to the flood mitigation projects already underway in Kolonnawa and Oliyumulla areas

<!-- p. 60 -->

associated with the city of Colombo, several other key initiatives are slated to commence in 2027. These include the 'Mudun Ela' project (aimed at flood prevention in the Wattala, Kelaniya, and Biyagama areas), a project to upgrade the stormwater drainage system in Piliyandala and Kesbewa, as well as the rehabilitation projects for the St. Sebastian Canal and the Kirulapana Canal, both designed to control flooding in the North Colombo region.

• Additionally, there are plans to develop a section of the Hamilton Canal. Furthermore, projects to improve the 'Bindunu Ela' (canal) system in Wadduwa and Molligoda (Kalutara District) as well as rainwater drainage systems in the Gampaha and Ratnapura districts, and in Dikwella, Beruwala, and Rathgama are set to commence in 2027.

• Rs. 3,200 million has been allocated for projects aimed at improving urban flood control and environmental systems, including the restoration of identified canals in Colombo and its suburban cities and the improvement of the stormwater drainage system adjacent to the Piliyandala–Kesbewa by-road.

• Furthermore, the ongoing Malwathu Oya projects will provide a solution to the flooding issues in the Mannar District. Additionally, preliminary work on the Mundeni Aru project, which directly addresses flooding concerns in the Ampara and Batticaloa districts is expected to commence next year.

Accordingly, an allocation of Rs. 7,700 million has been set aside for rainwater management. • Rs. 900 million has been allocated to carry out a scientific study on the sustainable development of the Beira Lake and the surrounding land use and prepare a development plan, and in the meantime to implement the essential development activities already identified.

• I emphasized in the last Budget the need for a sustainable solution for urban flood control. Rs. 250 million was allocated to prepare a short-term, medium- term and long-term plan for this purpose. That plan is currently being prepared.
