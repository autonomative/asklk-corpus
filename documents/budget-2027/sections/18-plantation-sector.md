# 18. Plantation Sector

<!-- p. 42 -->

It is expected to increase the export earnings of USD 3 billion currently generated by the plantation sector to USD 5 billion by 2030. For this, Rs. 13,000 million has been allocated for the development of the plantation sector in 2027. • Rs. 730 million has been allocated to implement the programme to establish 500 tea villages covering the Southern, Sabaragamuwa, North Western, Western, Uva and Central Provinces through an integrated approach, aimed at increasing the overall productivity of the smallholder tea sector, which contributes around 70 percent of tea production.

• In addition to the Rs. 500 million allocated for the rehabilitation of abandoned mature rubber plantations that are not tapped, provisions have been allocated for increasing rubber productivity in non-traditional areas by introducing suitable water management technologies, carrying out research to identify the commercial uses of rubber wood, and improving laboratory infrastructure related to the rubber industry.

• Rs. 2,500 million has been allocated for the Coconut Rehabilitation Project to increase the productivity of low-productivity coconut estates. • Rs. 100 million has been allocated to implement a programme for the sustainable transformation of Sri Lanka's palmyrah industry through improving the value chain of the palmyrah industry, promoting Small and Medium-sized Enterprises (SMEs) and innovation, centred on the Northern,

<!-- p. 43 -->

Eastern, Southern (Hambantota district) and North Western (Puttalam district) Provinces. • Rs. 3,000 million has been allocated for infrastructure development, including the construction of field rest rooms, the improvement of toilet facilities and the rehabilitation of Praja Shakthi centres, with the aim of increasing the productivity of the plantation workforce.

• The 2027 Budget has allocated Rs. 820 million to provide subsidies to small- scale tea estate owners for the replanting of tea and the infilling of vacancies on their lands. • It is proposed to allocate Rs. 5,000 million to provide a subsidy of Rs. 4,000 per bag of fertilizer obtained by small-scale tea holders, in order to ensure the continuous application of fertilizer in tea cultivation.

• Rs. 650 million has been allocated to encourage rubber growers, provide concessions for new planting and replanting, and promote rubber cultivation in non-traditional areas. • It is proposed to allocate Rs. 1,000 million to provide a fertilizer subsidy of Rs.

4,000 per bag, with the aim of ensuring the continued application of fertilizer in rubber plantations. • Provisions have been allocated to continue in 2027 the incentive allowance of Rs. 200 per day provided to estate workers last year. • The lease agreements for Regional Plantation Companies are set to expire in the year 2045. The Cabinet has appointed a committee of officials to review these agreements, with the objective of safeguarding the rights of both estate owners and employees, while also analyzing the productivity of the respective estates.
