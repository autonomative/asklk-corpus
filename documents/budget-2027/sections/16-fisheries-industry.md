# 16. Fisheries Industry

<!-- p. 36 -->

The fisheries industry is one of the most important industries, ensuring the country's food security, meeting a large percentage of the domestic protein requirement and uplifting the livelihoods of the fishing community. We expect to make the fisheries sector a more productive and sustainable sector through a modern approach based on modern technology, scientific management and the welfare of fishing communities. Accordingly, it has been planned to increase our export earnings of USD 300 million to USD 600 million by 2030. Rs. 6,200 million has been allocated for uplifting the fisheries industry, including the development of fishery harbours, strengthening new research relating to the fisheries industry at NARA, and promoting the inland fisheries industry.

• It is proposed to allocate Rs. 5,000 million for the development of the infrastructure required for the fisheries industry in the Northern Province, anchorages and the Pesalai Fishery Harbour. • Rs. 300 million has been allocated for the development of the Chilaw and Wellamankaraya fishery harbours.

• Rs. 1,500 million has been allocated for the development of the Gandara Fishery Harbour. • The Peliyagoda Fish Market is currently facing many problems, such as the absence of a formal waste disposal mechanism, inadequate cold storage

<!-- p. 37 -->

facilities and inadequate sanitation facilities. It is proposed to provide Rs. 1,000 million as equity capital to resolve these issues. It is expected to manage this centre under a new business structure. • It is proposed to provide provisions of Rs. 500 million as equity capital to the North Sea Limited, which manufactures and repairs net gear, to purchase the machinery required to strengthen new technical facilities and to construct a new net manufacturing and repair centre.

• It is proposed to allocate Rs. 300 million for the development of the Aquaculture Development Centre in Polonthalawa, which currently carries out development activities relating to the inland fisheries industry. • It is proposed to provide Rs. 200 million to the Ceylon Fisheries Corporation as equity capital for the development of the sales outlets owned by the Ceylon Fisheries Corporation.

• Pilot projects have confirmed that cage fish farming in inland reservoirs yields higher harvests. It is proposed to allocate Rs. 250 million to provide concessions to fishing communities to further popularize cage fish farming. • Rs. 300 million has been allocated for carrying out development needs assessments and feasibility studies targeting selected large-scale projects in the fisheries sector.

• Rs. 1,000 million has been allocated for sustainable development projects for fisheries resource management through ocean surveys, habitat mapping, fish stock assessments, the collection of monitoring data, laboratory analyses and ecosystem restoration activities.

• It is proposed to increase the compensation paid in the event of the sudden death of a fisherman engaged in the fisheries industry due to an accident while engaged in the industry from Rs. 1 million to Rs. 2 million.
