# 4. Strengthening the Legal Framework

<!-- p. 11 -->

A new and strong legal framework and institutional structure are required to achieve the economic transformation we envisage and to enhance efficiency. Some of our laws are more than 100 years old. The historical role of some of our institutions has come to an end. Those laws and institutions have become shackles that bind our feet from reaching the economic transformation we envisage.

Therefore, in order to achieve the new economic transformation, it is necessary to

<!-- p. 12 -->

reformulate old and outdated laws and institutions so that they can face present- day challenges, and to build new laws and institutions that correspond to the new circumstances that have arisen. Hence, we cannot move forward without removing these shackles. Accordingly;

• The "Rescue, Rehabilitation and Insolvency (Corporate and Personal) Act, No. 12 of 2026" has been passed to provide an opportunity for businesses that collapsed due to various crises such as pandemics, natural disasters and terrorist acts to rebuild, contribute to the economy and repay their creditors.

• The laws required to strengthen the prevention of money laundering have been passed through the "Prevention of Money Laundering (Amendment) Act, No. 16 of 2026". • The "Financial Transactions Reporting (Amendment) Act, No. 17 of 2026" has made provisions to minimize the risk arising from transactions not being reported to the financial system and to enforce the law against non-compliers.

• Through the "Convention on the Suppression of Terrorist Financing (Amendment) Act, No. 18 of 2026", the financing of global terrorism using Sri Lanka, which poses a threat to national security, has been prevented by strengthening the financial system. • Many people who have obtained microfinance loans are still stranded in a severe debt trap. For their protection, the "Microfinance and Credit Regulatory Authority Act, No. 9 of 2026" has already been passed. Accordingly, the 'Microfinance and Credit Regulatory Authority' will be established in 2027 and steps will be taken to provide protection to business customers.

• The "Public-Private Partnership (PPP) Act" has already been published in the Gazette for the initiation, development, management and monitoring of public-private partnership projects. It is expected to be presented to Parliament in early 2027. • The draft "State Owned Business Enterprises Management Bill" has already been referred to the Attorney General to incorporate a holding company for the efficient management of companies in which the State holds shares. 34 State-owned enterprises have already been identified for this purpose.

• The draft "Investment Protection Bill" has already been referred to the Attorney General to promote and protect investments and assets belonging to investors,

<!-- p. 13 -->

to ensure fair and equitable treatment, and to make provisions relating to the payment of compensation and the settlement of disputes. • The draft "Colombo Port City Economic Commission (Amendment) Bill" has been referred to the Attorney General to attract foreign investment to the Colombo Port City and to provide greater facilities for investors and those employed there.

• The "Institute of Professional Actuaries of Sri Lanka Act" required to establish a new institute aimed at ensuring the quality of professional actuaries and improving professional training is being drafted. • Data and information are most important in policy decision-making in this era.

The "National Statistics Act" is being drafted for the preparation, coordination and governance of official statistics in Sri Lanka. • The "Customs Ordinance (Amendment) Act" is expected to be passed in the first half of 2027 with the aim of making the Sri Lanka Customs more efficient and transparent and facilitating businesses. From October this year, Sri Lanka Customs has implemented a paperless programme and has digitalized the 'Customs Long Room', one of its divisions that had been in use for about a century.

• The "Financial Reporting Regulatory (Amendment) Act" will be amended to formalize the Sri Lanka Accounting Standards, Sri Lanka Auditing Standards and the regulatory standards on quality control. • The "Excise (Special Provisions) Act" will be amended to provide solutions to long-standing issues that have arisen in its implementation.

• The "Finance Business Act" will be amended to strengthen the regulation and supervision of finance companies, the investigation and prosecution of unauthorized finance businesses, and the requirements for winding up non- viable finance businesses. • The "Public Asset Management Act" will be introduced to meet the requirements of public asset management.

• The Cabinet of Ministers has appointed an expert committee to reform labour laws that have been in place for decades, so as to align them with current social and economic needs. This initiative aims to create a conducive working environment required for investment and production processes, to be carried

<!-- p. 14 -->

out in an effective and efficient manner and to promote employer-employee relations in which professional responsibility and accountability are clearly defined. This committee is in the process of formulating recommendations, taking into account the views and proposals of relevant stakeholders, including the National Labour Advisory Council.

Essential Structural Changes to be Made • An officials' committee has been appointed by the Cabinet of Ministers to look into minimizing delays in providing Government lands to investors, and to submit recommendations on the concessions to be granted according to the national importance of the project, the number of jobs generated and the area in which the investment is made.

• It is expected to establish a company, 100 percent owned by the Treasury, comprising professionals with private sector and international experience, for the promotion of investment. It is expected to provide this company too with market-competitive salaries and allowances, as was considered in the establishment of the GovTech institution.

• The Gambling Regulatory Authority has already been established to regulate casinos, online gambling and other games that fall into the category of gambling. The necessary regulations have already been finalized with expert assistance. • The National Single Window for Trade, which has been launched as a pilot project from the fifth of this month for the export of several selected goods, is expected to connect exporters as the first phase in the first half of 2027, and importers and re-exports in the second half of 2027. Rs. 1,990 million has been allocated for this purpose.

• Bids have been called to obtain a consultancy firm for the creation of the National Single Window for Investment, and these bids are currently being evaluated. Rs. 100 million has been allocated for its establishment. • It is proposed to allocate Rs. 1,000 million for the procurement of body scanners and container scanners, aimed at expanding the scanning system to strengthen the border protection process of Sri Lanka Customs.

• It is proposed to modernize the existing "Temporary Importation for Export Processing" (TIEP) process, under which goods imported for export are cleared

<!-- p. 15 -->

with tax concessions, in order to introduce "inter-port trading" mechanisms and to direct small and medium-scale industrialists towards exports.
