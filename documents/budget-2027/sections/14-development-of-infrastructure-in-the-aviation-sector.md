# 14. Development of Infrastructure in the Aviation Sector

<!-- p. 32 -->

## 14.1 Development of International Airports

At present, although the passenger capacity of the first terminal of the Bandaranaike International Airport is around 6 million, a larger number of passengers is being handled. It has been forecast that a capacity of 16 million will be required by 2032. Accordingly, increasing the passenger capacity of the airport has already been delayed. Therefore, we consider its development a priority.

Accordingly; • Work to increase the capacity of the first terminal from 6 million to 9 million has already commenced with an investment of Rs. 15,000 million by Airport and Aviation Services.

<!-- p. 33 -->

• Construction work on the delayed second terminal is scheduled to commence very soon with the assistance of the Japan International Cooperation Agency (JICA). • Construction of a Domestic Air Terminal within the airport premises will commence next year by Airport and Aviation Services (Sri Lanka) Limited (AASL) with an investment of Rs. 800 million, to enable international air passengers to travel more conveniently to domestic destinations.

• A feasibility study will be carried out for the widening of the road in front of the airport and the construction of a flyover to minimize traffic congestion at and around the airport. It is proposed to allocate Rs. 200 million for this purpose. • A new car park will be constructed by AASL at a cost of Rs. 400 million to minimize vehicle congestion in and around the airport.

• A re-export hub will be developed within the airport on a Build-Operate- Transfer (BOT) basis to transform the Bandaranaike International Airport into an air cargo handling hub, with the participation of the private sector as well. • The Ratmalana International Airport will be developed for pilot training, and domestic, business and regional aviation. To ensure aviation safety, a project of Rs. 6,000 million to rehabilitate the existing runway and extend it from 1,400 metres to 1,771 metres will commence in 2027 with the funds of AASL.

• The Mattala International Airport, which was constructed with a loan of USD 190 million and commenced flight operations in 2013, has today recorded an accumulated loss of over Rs. 70,000 million. Moreover, this loss grows annually. Therefore, Expressions of Interest (EOI) have already been called and concluded for the development of the areas of airport operations, data centres, food and catering services, and aircraft services and maintenance jointly with international companies. Steps are being taken to call for Requests for Proposals (RFP) from the selected companies. We expect to select new investors for this within the next year.

## 14.2 Domestic Airports

• It is proposed to allocate provisions of Rs. 100 million for improving the facilities, including the passenger terminal, of the Sigiriya Domestic Airport for the promotion of tourism centred in Sigiriya.

<!-- p. 34 -->

• The necessary investments for improving the facilities, including the passenger terminal, of the China Bay Airport will be made by AASL in 2027.
