# 17. Agriculture

<!-- p. 37 -->

The main livelihoods of rural people are bound up with farming. Although agriculture accounts for 23.9 percent of the labour force and around 39 percent of land use, and more than Rs. 200 billion has been spent annually on public investment and subsidies over the past few years, its contribution to the national economy is close to 8.4 percent. These data themselves demonstrate the problem

<!-- p. 38 -->

of farming life. It will not be possible to emerge from this crisis without subjecting this sector to a radical new transformation. • Private sector investment will be encouraged to increase large-scale agricultural production and its quality, improve the efficiency of agricultural value chains and expand markets.

• It is proposed to give paddy land owners the freedom to use uncultivated or low-productivity paddy lands, after they have been identified through an accurate survey, for field crops or permanent crops. Rs. 1,550 million has been allocated to encourage cultivation on these selected lands.

• A collective "Yaya" production approach will be introduced, instead of individual cultivation, for purchasing agricultural inputs in bulk, carrying out pest and disease control jointly, using water resources with maximum efficiency, facing price monopolies in the market and increasing productivity.

Rs. 400 million has been allocated as an initial step. • For rural agricultural development, Agrarian Service Centres will be transformed into 'One-Stop Shops' through the establishment of digital infrastructure, the creation of machinery pools, the empowerment of farming communities and the establishment of a digital platform through which real- time information can be exchanged. Rs. 250 million has been allocated to launch this as a pilot project in 5 main agricultural districts.

• It is proposed to allocate an additional sum of Rs. 300 million in addition to Rs. 700 million already allocated for food security to enable cooperative societies which have been already selected to receive paddy dryers in 2026 and possess the necessary machinery capacity to purchase paddy from the Paddy Marketing Board and release it to the market. Furthermore, priority will be given to meeting the working capital requirements of these societies, under an approved framework to facilitate the immediate commencement of these operations. This allocation is also intended to assist these cooperative societies in purchasing the machinery required for rice production.

• An Integrated Plant Nutrient Management System (IPNM) is required to promote the integrated use of chemical fertilizer and organic fertilizer, increase crop productivity and strengthen soil quality and nutrient status. It is proposed to implement a 4-year project for this purpose, and Rs. 47 million has been allocated for the year 2027 as an initial step.

<!-- p. 39 -->

• It is expected to provide the fertilizer subsidy of Rs. 30,000 per hectare for paddy cultivation, and Rs. 44,000 million has been allocated for this purpose. This subsidy of Rs. 30,000 per hectare of paddy cultivation will also be provided for other crops cultivated in paddy fields from the 2026/27 Maha season.

• Rs. 400 million has been allocated for the development of the entire value chain, aimed at enhancing the productivity, quality and global market access of the cinnamon industry.

## 17.1 Livestock

• It is expected to develop and maintain identified farms of the National Livestock Development Board as "Breeding Farms". Rs. 1,800 million has been allocated for the importation of foundation breeding animal stocks of high genetic quality required to establish specialized Nucleus Herds of "dairy cattle and buffaloes" at the Nikaweratiya Farm, "pigs" at the Horakele Farm, and "sheep" at the Siringapatha Farm.

• Provisions have been allocated for a feasibility study to establish a special economic zone, centred on the Western and North Western Provinces, for the export of chicken and chicken-related value-added products to foreign markets. • Provisions of Rs. 3,000 million were provided in 2026 to recommence the construction of the new Milco milk processing factory at Badalgama, on which EUR 57 million had been spent but which had been suspended for a long time.

Rs. 3,000 million has been allocated for the year 2027 to complete its work and establish the Milco factory there. This project, commenced in 2015 and was to be completed in 2019, has given rise to many problems due to its suspension for a long period, such as damage to machinery and construction, additional rehabilitation costs, increases in the prices of machinery parts, and the submission of delay claims by the contractor. Milco has already commenced discussions with the contractor, DESMI of Denmark, to resolve these issues.

• It is proposed to allocate a further Rs. 300 million, in addition to the Rs. 200 million currently allocated, to provide concessions to farmers for the

<!-- p. 40 -->

establishment of goat farms in districts including Puttalam, Mannar and Batticaloa.

## 17.2 Irrigation Sector

Developing and maintaining a strong irrigation system is essential to increase agricultural production, ensure food security, strengthen the livelihoods of rural people and efficiently manage water resources in the face of climate change. Through this Budget, Rs. 77,000 million has been allocated, providing adequate and prioritized financial provisions for irrigation development, to accelerate ongoing irrigation development activities and to commence new projects.

• Rs. 21,000 million has been estimated for the rehabilitation of the cascaded tank systems damaged by "Ditwah" and the associated minor irrigation infrastructure and agricultural roads. These programmes commenced in 2026 with the Ditwah provisions, and the remaining provisions required will be provided in 2027.

• Although a feasibility study on the Jaffna Irrigation Project was carried out in 2018 to improve the water security of the people of the Jaffna Peninsula, it has not been implemented to date. Rs. 75 million has been allocated for further feasibility studies, including on this project.

• Rs. 7,500 million has been allocated to accelerate the work of the Lower Malwathu Oya Project, construction of which has already commenced, for the agricultural and social development of the Anuradhapura, Vavuniya and Mannar districts. • It is proposed to allocate Rs. 1,000 million to meet the water requirements of the Batticaloa and Ampara districts, minimize flood risk and commence the Mundeni Aru River Basin Development Project.

• The water potential of the Hambantota industrial zone and its associated areas will be around 115 million cubic metres by 2040. There is an additional water requirement of approximately 115 million cubic metres (MCM) per year for domestic and industrial needs. To find solutions to this requirement, water sources including the Rakwana Ganga, the additional effective water capacity of the Samanalawewa Reservoir and the Uma Oya water transfer to the Kirindi Oya basin have been identified, and the necessary provisions have been

allocated to carry out an integrated water balance and pre-feasibility study on them. • Rs. 5,000 million has been allocated for irrigation projects such as the implementation of the Wayamba Canal Project to divert surplus water from the Weemedilla Reservoir to the Mee Oya basin in the Kurunegala district, the modernization of the over 50-year-old Rajanganaya lift irrigation scheme, the completion of work on the halted feeder canal from the Siyambalangamuwa Oya to the Mahagalgamuwa Oya, and the mitigation of floods in Gampaha town.

• Rs. 19,500 million has been allocated for the Mahaweli Water Security and Investment Programme, including the North Central Province Greater Canal Project. • It is expected to carry out feasibility studies to recommence the Kumbukkan Oya Reservoir Project aimed at meeting the water requirements of the Moneragala district and the Heda Oya Reservoir Development Project aimed at improving the water supply of the Ampara district. It is proposed to allocate an additional Rs. 200 million for the preliminary work of such irrigation projects.

## 17.3 Lands

• Rs. 800 million has been allocated in 2027 to further strengthen the ongoing Bimsaviya programme. • Programmes for the registration of land titles, the digitalization of land records and the modernization of land information systems will also be continued, and Rs. 140 million has been allocated for this purpose.

• It is also expected to implement in 2027 the programme to digitalize the plans relating to 3.5 million land parcels held in hard copy by the Survey Department and integrate them with the land information system. Provisions have been allocated for this purpose.
