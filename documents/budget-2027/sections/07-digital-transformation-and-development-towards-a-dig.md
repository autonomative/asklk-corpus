# 7. Digital Transformation and Development towards a Digital Era

<!-- p. 18 -->

**Honourable Speaker,**

<!-- p. 19 -->

Digital transformation is no longer merely a technological initiative, but a fundamental requirement for improving the efficiency of the public sector, strengthening governance, facilitating economic growth, and ensuring that every citizen can access Government services. Digitalization has been identified as a fundamental tool for modernizing public administration, improving the efficiency and transparency of Government activities, and strengthening institutional capacity.

Reaching the level of development this sector has attained in today's world is a challenging and multifaceted process. At the same time, this sector is constantly evolving. Therefore, in order to reap its true benefits, the necessary legal and institutional structure must first be built.

• The GovTech institution has been established under the 100 percent ownership of the Treasury to guide and implement the digitalization of public services, and has already commenced its operations. • The Personal Data Protection Act has been amended and the Data Protection Authority has already been established and made operational.

• The Bill for the establishment of the Digital Economy Authority and the National Digital Economy Council (Digital Economy Bill) is expected to be passed in the first quarter of 2027. • The Cyber Security Bill, which is essential for protection with the growth of the digital sector, has been submitted to the Attorney General for clearance.

Accordingly, it is expected to establish a Cyber Security Authority under it in 2027. • There is a problematic situation in the Government procurement process when purchasing digital solutions and facilities. Therefore, Government procurement guidelines specific to digital solutions and facilities have been prepared, and discussions have already commenced with the Procurement Commission. Accordingly, it will be possible to introduce these procurement guidelines in the first half of 2027.

For a new transformation in the digital economy sector; • Establishing a National Artificial Intelligence Centre of Excellence at Wijerama Mawatha, bringing the Government, the expert community and industry

<!-- p. 20 -->

under one roof to build national AI capabilities and promote the responsible use of AI; • Establishing the "Shared Platform First and Buy Before Build Policy", which, in parallel with the standard for integrating public institutions through digital public infrastructure, gives priority to purchasing standardized solutions, and to shared use, before developing new software in software procurement;

• Expanding the Lanka Government Cloud (LGC) system from LGC 2.0 to LGC 3.0 and establishing the core foundation of sovereign infrastructure for Artificial Intelligence; • Digitalizing the services provided by Divisional Secretariats and Grama Niladharis so that citizens can easily access Government services;

• Launching the "Presidential Digital Excellence Awards" programme to appriase high-performing public institutions with the aim of rapidly promoting digitalization; • Adopting and expanding cashless payment methods such as GovPay and QR to steer towards a digital revenue economy;

• Further, preparing the necessary regulatory basis for Virtual Asset Service Providers (VASP) through the establishment of a formal legal framework; • Preparing sectoral digital plans to align the digitalization of key strategic sectors with the National Digital Economy Plan, and carrying out operations through institutional Digital Transformation Units (DTUs);

• Providing electricity tariff concessions and other incentives to encourage investors selected under formal criteria, with the expectation of encouraging investment in the establishment of data centres; • Providing broadband vouchers to 750,000 selected school students to enhance digital literacy among them;

• Strengthening the Digital Startup Fund of Funds to attract private investment into Sri Lankan businesses; It is proposed to allocate Rs. 10,000 million for the above proposals. Rs. 18,500 million has already been allocated to the Ministry of Digital Economy for the year 2027 to support the digital transformation and the development of the digital economy of the country under 6 key areas, namely 1) institutional

<!-- p. 21 -->

strengthening, policy and regulation, 2) digital Government transformation, 3) digital industry development and growth, 4) empowering the digital revenue economy, 5) empowering digital activities in strategic industries, and 6) promoting inclusive access and adoption, and capacity development, and this amount will be increased to Rs. 28,500 million.

• Regulations were formulated to remove the Cellular Tower Levy on new telecommunication towers for a period of 5 years, which was among the proposals I presented in the 2026 Budget. While expanding high-speed internet facilities within the country through the further successful implementation of that programme, there is a requirement for between 700 - 800 telecommunication towers to expand high-speed broadband internet coverage island-wide under the "Gamata Sannivedanaya" Programme by 2030. Of these, 573 telecommunication towers will be completed by 2028. A grant of 75 percent of the cost incurred for a new tower constructed or Rs. 35 million, whichever is lower, will be provided. In addition, Rs. 5 million will be provided for obtaining an electricity connection. Rs. 9,000 million has been allocated for this purpose by the Telecommunications Regulatory Commission through the "Gamata Sannivedanaya" Programme.

• At present, there are problems with the Quality of Service and Quality of Experience in the mobile (broadband) connectivity networks in the country, and rural people in particular face great difficulty. To identify and correct these accurately, the Telecommunications Regulatory Commission will commence the introduction of an automated monitoring system in 2027 for this purpose, for the first time in Sri Lanka, through which it is expected to improve the Quality of Service and Quality of Experience of the networks of mobile (broadband) connectivity providers in the country. With the aim of correcting the financial loss incurred by the country as a result of the frequency spectrum belonging to the people of this country being issued without effective and efficient management, the installation of an Automated Spectrum Management System and an Automated Spectrum Monitoring System will commence in 2027. The Telecommunications Regulatory Commission has allocated Rs. 3,100 million for the procurement of all these systems.

• Steps will be taken to rapidly expand the opportunity for citizens to obtain services without physically visiting Government institutions (presence-less services). Accordingly, from 2027 onwards, the range of priority public services

<!-- p. 22 -->

relating to licences, applications, approvals and payments that are widely obtained by the public, from the village level to Divisional Secretariats, Local Government institutions and the Central Government level, will be gradually expanded to be delivered through digital means. With the introduction of the National Digital Identity (Digital ID) and the expansion of its use in the future, it is expected to enhance these services rapidly and sustainably and to extend them across the entire Government machinery.

Our objective is to build an efficient public service and a competitive digital economy, while making the daily lives of the people easier through digital technology.
