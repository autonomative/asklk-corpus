# 20. Social Protection and Empowerment

<!-- p. 45 -->

The achievements of Sri Lanka in social development and social protection are at a high level compared to many developing countries. However, many new challenges are emerging within the country due to significant changes in household population composition and social dynamics. In particular, the decrease in the number of household members, the increase in the elderly population and the low level of female labour force participation are prominent among these.

• Rs. 230 billion has been allocated in 2027 for social protection allowances, including Aswesuma, to minimize economic hardships and ensure the social protection of the poor and extremely poor groups by continuing to provide them with Aswesuma benefits. • The monthly allowance provided to kidney patients will be increased by Rs.

2,500, from Rs. 10,000 to Rs. 12,500. • The monthly allowance provided to persons with disabilities will be increased by Rs. 2,500, from Rs. 10,000 to Rs. 12,500. • Rs. 25,000 million has been allocated to implement the integrated rural development programme island-wide under the Praja Shakthi National Movement, giving priority attention to the eradication of poverty.

• An empowerment pilot project was implemented with the assistance of projects funded by the Asian Development Bank and the World Bank, and Rs. 4,500 million has been allocated for the programme to systematically empower eligible families with potential. • Rs. 182 million has been allocated for the holistic personality development of children through a family-centred approach, with the aim of ensuring the protection, health, education and mental well-being of vulnerable children.

• Rs. 102 million has been allocated for an alternative care structure focused on vulnerable children and children who have lost their parents due to disasters.

<!-- p. 46 -->

• A Disability Inclusion Focal Point will be established in every Ministry, Department and major public institution for the community of persons with disabilities. • A sum of Rs. 150 million has been allocated under the National Secretariat for Persons with Disabilities to provide technical equipment, medical aids, or other facilities to individuals with disabilities who are otherwise unable to obtain them, thereby facilitating their daily lives. It is proposed that this program be implemented on a broader scale through coordination with the President’s Fund and other relevant institutions.

• The establishment of accessible digital public services will be made mandatory, that is, taking steps to design all public digital services in accordance with the W3C accessibility specifications. • It is proposed to allocate Rs. 1,000 million for a programme to be implemented by 'Clean Sri Lanka' to ensure physical accessibility within government institutions giving effect to a Supreme Court order and addressing the mobility needs of the elderly and persons with disabilities in the country. Furthermore, it is ensured that provisions will be made available to every institution that requests such support.

• Rs. 100 million has been allocated to develop the 'Pannala Welcome Village' elders' home under the National Secretariat for Elders as a model elderly care centre. • Rs. 75 million has been allocated for the construction of four new wards and a wastewater management system for homeless and destitute persons at the Rediyagama Kalana Detention Home.

• Day care centres will be constructed for children with disabilities, and Rs. 200 million has been allocated to provide transport facilities for children with disabilities. • It is proposed to allocate Rs. 100 million to extend State patronage for meeting the basic needs of care centres for persons with disabilities run by non- governmental organizations.

• It is proposed to allocate Rs. 500 million to provide facilities for high-capacity children's homes.

Sri Lankans Overseas • We must take a new approach to connect the knowledge and abilities of Sri Lankans living abroad and their children, as well as their bond to the motherland, to the future development journey of the country. Accordingly, we propose to introduce a "Global Sri Lankan Heritage Citizen" programme for the second and third generations of the overseas community of Sri Lankan origin. Accordingly, we propose to grant dual citizenship, free of any charge, to the spouse and children of an applicant for dual citizenship, as well as to third-generation children.

• A highly concessionary housing loan scheme of up to Rs. 5 million will be implemented to meet the housing needs of migrant workers. Under this scheme, steps will be taken to provide an interest subsidy of 10 percent for up to Rs. 3 million and an interest subsidy of 5 percent for the remaining Rs. 2 million.

It is proposed that, for the first Rs. 3 million, a contribution of 6 percent be borne by the Government and a contribution of 4 percent by the Sri Lanka Bureau of Foreign Employment, and for the remaining Rs. 2 million, a contribution of 3 percent be borne by the Government and a contribution of 2 percent by the Sri Lanka Bureau of Foreign Employment.

It is proposed to allocate a sum of Rs. 1,000 million in 2027 as the Government contribution for the implementation of this interest subsidy scheme.
