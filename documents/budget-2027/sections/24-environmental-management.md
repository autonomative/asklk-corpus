# 24. Environmental Management

<!-- p. 52 -->

It is our duty to safeguard environmental balance for sustainable economic development. It is one of our fundamental responsibilities to secure the natural resources of the country for future generations by facing the challenges of climate change, protecting biodiversity and moving towards a green economy approach.

• Our Government, which has understood the importance of the wildlife sector, has recruited 313 wildlife guards and 3,451 electric fence maintenance assistants to strengthen the services of the Department of Wildlife Conservation for its protection and management. Provisions have been allocated for the recruitment of 114 wildlife rangers. Vehicles and equipment, including 16 double cabs, 84 single cabs, 181 motorcycles and 09 tractors, have been added in 2026 for field operations to deliver wildlife services efficiently.

• With the aim of minimizing the human-elephant conflict and protecting human lives and wild elephants, the Handapanagala, Koholankala and Wetahira Kanda elephant corridors were released in June 2026, and it is expected to declare the elephant corridor located between the Nachchaduwa Wewa and Maha Kanadarawa Wewa as a wildlife reserve.

• Rs. 250 million has been allocated for the construction of new electric fences and the rehabilitation of existing electric fences. In addition, it is expected to allocate provisions of Rs. 200 million for this purpose from the Wildlife Fund as well. • A pilot project has been launched using Artificial Intelligence (AI) and thermal cameras to minimize accidents caused by wild elephants colliding with trains.

Accordingly, it has been possible to reduce the number of such deaths this year. Based on its results, it is expected to implement this project nationally. • A land area of 03 acres has been identified within the Nuwaragam Forest Reserve in the Anuradhapura district to construct a hospital capable of treating wild animals for animal welfare. It is expected to allocate Rs. 700 million from the Wildlife Fund for the establishment of this hospital.

• Stranded baby elephants from all areas are currently rehabilitated at the Udawalawe Elephant Transit Home, and their lives are at risk due to the need to transport the baby elephants over very long distances. Therefore, it is expected to provide estimated provisions of Rs. 5,000 million in several phases

<!-- p. 53 -->

from the Wildlife Fund for the construction of a new elephant transit home on a land area of 15 acres in the Polonnaruwa district. • Rs. 50 million has been allocated from the Wildlife Fund for the initial work on constructing a centre for the treatment and rehabilitation of injured marine animals such as turtles and dolphins on a land area of 2 acres in the Dodanduwa area.

We expect to further strengthen the legal framework to protect the wild elephant resource, which is a living heritage of Sri Lanka, and to prevent the cruelty inflicted on them. • It is proposed to increase the compensation paid for damage to a house due to the human-elephant conflict from Rs. 200,000 to Rs. 1 million, the compensation paid to a person who dies or becomes fully disabled due to attacks by protected animals, including wild elephants, from Rs. 1 million to Rs. 2 million, the compensation paid for partial disability from Rs. 150,000 to Rs. 500,000, and the compensation paid for other property other than houses from Rs. 200,000 to Rs. 500,000.

• It is proposed to amend the Fauna and Flora Protection Ordinance to increase, in addition to the existing term of imprisonment, the fine for killing, hunting, shooting, wounding or capturing a wild elephant from the current fine of Rs. 150,000 to Rs. 500,000 to Rs. 500,000 to Rs. 1 million. It is proposed to amend the Increase of Fines Act, No. 12 of 2005 to increase the fine for illegally keeping an elephant in possession from the current Rs. 150,000 to Rs. 250,000 to Rs.

500,000 to Rs. 1 million. • Animals may face a water shortage due to the drying up of tanks within wildlife reserves. To face this situation, funds will be allocated through the Wildlife Conservation Fund for the rehabilitation in 2027 of the tanks currently identified by the Department of Wildlife Conservation as requiring rehabilitation. Further, an additional Rs. 90 million has been allocated for this purpose from the 2027 Budget.

• In addition to the above provisions, it is proposed to allocate Rs. 5,000 million as advance preparedness to meet the requirements that may arise from the El Niño risk expected in the future.

<!-- p. 54 -->

• Provisions have already been allocated to obtain vessels and machinery worth Rs. 3,380 million under Japanese cooperation to strengthen the capacity to respond promptly to marine and coastal pollution caused by oil spills, in order to protect the environment of the coastal belt.

• Rs. 800 million has been allocated for 10 new sustainable environmental management projects, including the protection and stability of the eastern coastal belt, coastal protection in the Karukupane area in the Puttalam district, and the establishment of an ambient air quality monitoring network.

• The protection of the Central Highlands, which are under severe threat, is the protection of the future of our nation. For this, it is proposed to establish an Upper Watershed Area Management Authority to establish an integrated watershed management approach and for restoration activities. It is expected to present the relevant Act to Parliament very soon. Discussions have already commenced to obtain loan assistance of USD 100 million for the functions to be assigned to this Authority.
