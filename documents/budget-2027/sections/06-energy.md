# 6. Energy

<!-- p. 15 -->

A transformative change in the energy sector is required for the future economic transformation, national security and the improvement of the living standards of the people. The plans required for a new transformation in the energy sector, aimed at ensuring a stable and uninterrupted energy supply and reducing production costs, have been included in this Budget.

## 6.1 Electricity

Today, around 62 percent of our installed capacity comes from renewable energy. Plans have been prepared to increase this to 69 percent by 2029. As energy sources that vary with weather and seasonal conditions, such as solar and wind power, are increasingly added, we must pay greater attention to the stability control of the system. The national power system failures that occurred in

<!-- p. 16 -->

February 2025 and October 2026 were an important lesson for us to reflect on our system protection, frequency control, power flow management and network protection mechanisms. We expect to make an investment of Rs. 26.5 billion to technically strengthen our grid system so that such incidents do not recur.

• The installation of two synchronous condensers of 75 MVA (megavolt- amperes) each at the new Habarana and Mannar locations, which will help maintain grid stability when renewable energy is added to the national system at a high percentage, and the installation of STATCOM facilities with a capacity of 300 MVAr at the Padukka 220 kV grid substation, are expected to commence in 2027 and be completed in 2029 with the financial assistance of the World Bank.

• A Wide Area Monitoring System will be established under the "World Bank Technical Assistance Project" to identify unstable conditions in the system in advance and to make prompt and effective decisions in emergencies. • We have already introduced Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS) to store the surplus electricity we receive during the daytime. In 2026, 160 MW is received by the system through BESS. Plans have been made for this quantity to be 560 MW in 2027 and for the total quantity to be 1,245 MW by 2029.

• The price selected in the first phase (160 MW) of calling bids for the BESS system in 2026 was Rs. 2.033 million per megawatt per month. In the second phase in 2026, when bids were called for 250 megawatts, the bid received had decreased to Rs. 1.808 million per megawatt per month. Accordingly, through competitive bidding, it has been possible to reduce the price by around 11 percent within this year itself.

• With the aim of promoting renewable energy generation, enhancing energy security, and ensuring stability by minimizing imbalances in the national power system, it is proposed to provide a subsidy for the purchase of the batteries required for energy storage to domestic consumers who install rooftop solar power systems equipped with battery storage facilities under the Net Plus scheme.

• Domestic consumers who newly install rooftop solar power systems will be provided a subsidy of Rs. 25,000 per kilowatt of battery storage capacity. It is proposed to provide this subsidy for up to 10 kilowatts, subject to a maximum of Rs. 250,000. It is proposed to provide Rs. 3,500 million for this purpose. It is

<!-- p. 17 -->

also proposed to remove the Ports and Airports Development Levy (PAL) charged on the importation of batteries used for the storage of solar energy. • Although the need for supplementary power supply in the event of an emergency at the Norochcholai Lakvijaya Power Plant has been discussed for a long time, no appropriate steps have been taken to address it. Measures are now being taken to establish a BESS with a capacity of 30 MW to provide this supplementary power.

• Rs. 500 million has been allocated for the preliminary work of the 400 kV Northern main transmission network from Kilinochchi to New Habarana, with the aim of completing it by 2030. We expect to obtain financial assistance of USD 150 million from the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB) to construct the transmission line.

• It is expected to invite private investments for the construction of 2 transmission lines of 220 kV from Norochcholai to Wariyapola and from Moneragala to Hambantota, and the New Habarana–Kirindiwela 400 kV transmission line. • In 2027, smart meters will be provided free of charge to 300,000 electricity consumers who use more than 180 units, on a grant of USD 12 million from the Asian Development Bank (ADB). Smart meters will be provided to all electricity consumers who use more than 120 units in the areas served by Lanka Electricity Company (LECO).

• A new capacity of 370 MW generated from wind power will be added to the system by 2029. Of this, steps will be taken to add 50 MW in 2027. By the end of 2029, the installed capacity generated from wind power will increase to 674 MW. • Ten reservoirs, including Randenigala, Deduru Oya, Ridiyagama, Ambalan Oya, Castlereigh, Chandrika Wewa and Kiri Ibban Ara, have been selected for the installation of floating solar panels. We expect to generate around 500 MW of electricity by installing solar panels using 10 percent or less of the water surface of these reservoirs. These projects will be carried out by the Government and the private sector. Steps will be taken in January 2027 to call for investment proposals from the private sector.

<!-- p. 18 -->

• Four Distribution Control Centres will be established in Kuliyapitiya, Peradeniya, Battaramulla and Dehiwala to manage power failures, optimize the power grid, manage electricity demand, integrate and manage solar energy resources and other distributed energy resources into the power system, and strengthen data-driven planning of electricity distribution systems.

## 6.2 Petroleum

• The Petroleum Development Authority of Sri Lanka (PDASL) has called for bids for oil and natural gas exploration and production (Sri Lanka Licencing Round 2026) in 4 blocks in the Mannar Basin, located off the western coast. The bids are expected to be opened on February 2, 2027.

• To increase fuel storage capacity, 6 tanks with a capacity of 64,000 m3 are being constructed at the Kolonnawa Petroleum Complex with an investment of Rs. 3,746 million in 2025, 2 tanks with a capacity of 22,000 m3 with an investment of Rs. 1,446 million at the end of this year, and 3 tanks with a capacity of 40,000 m3 at the Muthurajawela Storage Terminal with an investment of Rs. 3,786 million in September 2026. Accordingly, our storage capacity will increase by 126,000 m3, up to the requirement for 45 days.

• Bids have been called for the construction of a pipeline system from Kolonnawa to the Port with an investment of Rs. 16,332 million, with the aim of reducing the time taken to unload oil from ships from 96 hours to 48 hours. This is expected to be completed within 18 months.

• In line with the proposal to develop the Bandaranaike International Airport, Katunayake as a hub serving the international aviation sector, construction work has already commenced on a tank complex with a capacity of 92,000 m3 for Jet A1 fuel at the Muthurajawela Petroleum Terminal and a pipeline to carry aviation fuel from Muthurajawela to the Bandaranaike International Airport, Katunayake, with an investment of Rs. 22,340 million.

• The Sapugaskanda Oil Refinery will be upgraded from its current limit of 50,000 barrels per day to a capacity of 100,000 barrels under a public-private partnership. For this, Requests for Proposals (RFP) will be called in 2027 based on the Expressions of Interest (EOI) already called.
