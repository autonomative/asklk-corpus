# 22. Health Services

<!-- p. 48 -->

Capital expenditure of Rs. 131 billion has been allocated for maintaining and improving the public health service so that every citizen is able to obtain the quality health services they require through all medical systems administered by the central Government and the Provincial Councils.

• A large number of vacancies existed due to the non-recruitment of health staff over a long period. In order to deliver a quality health service, the required human resources have been recruited, namely 1,797 doctors, 303 indigenous medical practitioners, 64 dental surgeons, 3,441 nurses, 952 public health midwives and 3,415 support staff in various areas.

• The pilot project of 39 "Arogya Wellness Centres", aimed at providing patient- centred health services close to between 5,000 - 10,000 of the population, is being successfully implemented, and it is expected to construct 1,000 centres during the period 2027 – 2029 with an investment of Rs. 17,440 million jointly with the Provincial Councils. Rs. 3,600 million has been allocated for the year 2027 for this purpose.

• In response to the requests of the people of those areas and to provide an optimal service at the level of national hospitals, it is proposed to transfer to the central Government the District General Hospitals of Mannar, Mullaitivu, Vavuniya, Kilinochchi, Gampaha and Avissawella, which are administered by the Provincial Councils, out of the 20 District General Hospitals.

• It is proposed to allocate Rs. 300 million for the construction of an Accident and Emergency Treatment Unit with modern facilities at the Thambuttegama Base Hospital. • Provisions of Rs. 65,587 million are required to fully meet, within 03 years, the requirement for high-end biomedical equipment identified through a formal

<!-- p. 49 -->

study, including MRI, CT and Cath Lab, which is necessary to enhance diagnostic and treatment services in all hospitals. For this, it is proposed to allocate Rs. 5,000 million in addition to the provisions of Rs. 15,500 million allocated for the year 2027. • There are 400 ambulances in the health service that are more than 20 years old, of which around 50 percent are more than 25 years old. In order to ensure a better quality and more efficient service to the people, it is proposed to allocate Rs. 3,000 million in 2027 to add new ambulances to the system in place of the old ambulances. In addition, 25 ambulances through Asian Development Bank loan assistance and 20 new ambulances as a grant from Japan, coordinated by the Asian Development Bank, will be added within the next year.

• 100 new ambulances will be added in 2027 under Indian assistance of Rs. 1,725 million to expand the 1990 Suwa Seriya service, which consists of 319 ambulances. • An organ transplant centre, including for the liver, at a cost of Rs. 3,155 million, will be established within the National Hospital of Colombo with the financial contribution of local and foreign institutions and organizations. Rs. 50 million has been allocated in 2027 for the initial activities.

• Rs. 400 million has been allocated to implement widely within the country the biological Wolbachia dengue vector control method, with the technical contribution of the World Mosquito Program (WMP) and the co-financing contribution of the Government, to control the dengue epidemic.

• It is proposed to allocate Rs. 100 million to provide spectacles free of charge to school children to minimize disruptions to education caused by visual impairments. • In addition to the development projects currently being implemented in hospitals providing secondary and tertiary care services, Rs. 1,245 million has been allocated to commence a number of new projects, including official quarters, at the Badulla and Batticaloa Teaching Hospitals, the Matale and Nawalapitiya District General Hospitals, and the Puttalam Base Hospital.

• Rs. 510 million has been allocated to prepare, in 2027, feasibility studies and preliminary plans relating to the identified development projects in hospitals, including the Matara District General Hospital, its relocation to the Kotawila

<!-- p. 50 -->

area in Kamburugamuwa, the Kurunegala, Kalubowila and Kuliyapitiya Teaching Hospitals, the Trincomalee Siddha Teaching Hospital, the Moneragala and Polonnaruwa District General Hospitals, the National Institute of Infectious Diseases and the Gampola Base Hospital, as well as to providing accommodation facilities for hospital staff and establishing regional medical supplies stores as required.

• It is proposed to establish a centre within the Faculty of Medicine, Ragama to train doctors in Robotic Surgery. It is expected to provide training for the work of this centre in collaboration with foreign universities. It is proposed to allocate Rs. 250 million for this purpose.

• The “Rabies-Free Sri Lanka by 2030” program is being implemented with the goal of eradicating canine rabies in the country by 2030, primarily through the administration of rabies vaccines to dogs and the streamlining of canine sterilization programs. It is proposed that this initiative be carried out as a collaborative effort, integrating the canine rabies control program managed under the Ministry of Health and Mass Media with technical support from the Department of Animal Production and Health, while also utilizing the infrastructure available within local government institutions. We intend to ensure that the allocations already provided to the institutions dealing with this matter are properly coordinated and implemented.
