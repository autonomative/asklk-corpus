# 31. Cultural and National Heritage

<!-- p. 63 -->

Our national identity has been established through all ethnicities and religions living in harmony as one family. It is one of our foremost responsibilities to formally conserve that heritage in order to bequeath it to future generations. For this, steps will be taken to digitalize and conserve sacred places, historical artefacts and cultural identities through digital technological innovations such as

<!-- p. 64 -->

3D Laser Scanning used internationally as well as locally. This will make it possible to permanently maintain accurate three-dimensional models of the national heritage even in the face of natural disasters or weather impacts. Rs. 7,000 million has been allocated in this Budget for the promotion and development of artistic activities associated with various ethnicities and religions.

• Anuradhapura is a sacred area of national and international importance in which many unique historical, archaeological, religious and cultural heritages of Sri Lanka are concentrated. It represents the historical heritage of Buddhist civilization. Therefore, we expect to implement a number of development activities, including improving the facilities required by devotees, road rehabilitation and waste management, while safeguarding its archaeological and religious values for future generations. An advisory council, co-chaired by the Acting Chief Prelate of the Atamasthana, has already been established to monitor and guide this programme. For this purpose, Rs. 1,076 million has already been allocated by the Central Cultural Fund for the year 2027. The Urban Development Authority has prepared an estimate of Rs. 8,200 million, and it is proposed to allocate Rs. 500 million for the year 2027.

• Funds have been allocated to the Ministry of Housing and Water Supply to implement the 'Yathiwara' housing program for parents, which was initiated in 2026, during the year 2027. • The Government has provided provisions of Rs. 722 million for the rehabilitation of 1,328 places of religious worship damaged by the Ditwah disaster, comprising 758 Buddhist temples, 272 Hindu places of worship, 199 Muslim places of worship and 99 Christian places of worship. In addition, among the 1,328 destroyed places of religious worship, Rs. 40 million from the Buddha Sasana Fund for 30 Buddhist temples, and Rs. 523 million from the Central Cultural Fund for archaeological sites, including places of religious worship, are being utilized for rehabilitation, and a temple is also being rehabilitated through the Anagarika Dharmapala Trust with funds donated by the Maha Bodhi Society of India.
