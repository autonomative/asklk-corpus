# 13. Port Development

<!-- p. 31 -->

Located at a highly strategic position on the East-West global sea route, the Port of Colombo holds 20th place among the ports of the world. Developing the port and its related services brings great importance to the country's economy. Our target is to increase its total shipping capacity to over 30 million Twenty-foot Equivalent Units (TEUs) by 2040, and to develop it as a re-export shipping and logistics hub.

• With financing from the Asian Development Bank and the World Bank, a 3.2 kilometre breakwater will be constructed for the West Terminal II and the development required for handling 3.2 million containers will be carried out. • The feasibility study currently being carried out for the development of the Colombo North Port is expected to be completed in the first half of next year.

It is expected to spend Rs. 300 million from the funds of the Ports Authority to expeditiously carry out the additional study on the Kelani River estuary, which is a decisive factor in the development of the North Port. • Requests for Proposals (RFP) for the establishment of a Logistics Centre in the Port of Colombo through a public-private partnership will be called within the next 2 months.

• It has been planned to provide reliable and efficient services by technically strengthening the Vessel Traffic Management System of the Port of Colombo. Provisions of Rs. 575 million have already been allocated to commence this project of Rs. 3,578 million, which is implemented with the assistance of the Government of Japan.

• Bunkering Capacity With the number of vessels arriving at Sri Lankan ports increasing day by day, there is great potential to expand and strengthen bunkering capacity and to establish Sri Lanka as a competitive regional bunkering hub. Accordingly, with the support of the Ceylon Petroleum Corporation (CPC), it is expected to increase capacity by 50,000 metric tons within the Port of Colombo under the participation of the private sector on a Build-Operate-Transfer (BOT) basis.

<!-- p. 32 -->

o The Ceylon Petroleum Corporation and the private sector will work jointly to develop the storage and pipeline systems required for bunkering services centred on the Port of Galle. o There is potential to develop the Port of Trincomalee as a fuel storage hub as well as a strategic hub that can be connected to the energy supply network of the South Asian region. Steps will be taken to develop this jointly through State and international partnerships.

o Repair work on 2 of the 5 selected tanks in the Trincomalee Upper Tank Farm has already been completed. The construction of a new pipeline system from the tank farm to the port has been planned. The Ceylon Petroleum Corporation expects to spend Rs. 7,375 million for this purpose.

o It is expected to call for investment proposals (RFP) in 2027 to develop 29 of the 61 tanks owned by Trinco Petroleum Terminal (Private) Limited at the Port of Trincomalee, jointly established by the Ceylon Petroleum Corporation and Lanka IOC, under a public-private partnership.

We believe that once these development activities are implemented, Sri Lanka's maritime competitiveness will be significantly strengthened and the country will be established as a key energy hub in the global shipping and maritime industry, including offshore bunkering.
